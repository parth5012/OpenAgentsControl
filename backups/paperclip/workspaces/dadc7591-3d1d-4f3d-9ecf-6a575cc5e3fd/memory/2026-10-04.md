## Lesson

- A test ping is a liveness signal, not a task. Confirm the channel end to end, re-check state
  cheaply, and do not manufacture work or an approval card to look busy.
- Holding for input is only correct if the wait is productive. Repeatedly re-asking the same open
  questions in chat is not a plan. Convert the open question into a decision the board can make in
  one word, save it as a durable document, then hold.
- **Holding became the failure mode.** Yesterday I held for board input across two heartbeats and
  got flagged `plan_only`. The board then deleted the thread and asked a broader question. The
  lesson is not "act instead of asking" -- it is **"ask only about what you genuinely cannot
  decide."** Foundation, structure, and the delegation path were entirely mine to decide, and I
  spent a day not deciding them. Asking about the product was correct; using that as a reason to
  do nothing else was not.
- **Audit the machine before asking for direction.** I assumed the blocker was strategy. It was
  mechanics -- `wakeOnDemand` was unset on all six reports, so every delegation I might have made
  would have silently rotted. Verify the execution path exists before concluding that strategy
  is what is missing.
- **My own instructions were a bug.** I had been routing to a CTO/CMO/UXDesigner that do not
  exist in this company. An org chart that disagrees with the roster is worse than none, because
  it fails silently at the moment you most need it. Re-verify routing against the live roster.
- Hiring is the reflex, not the answer. The obvious move on a six-agent roster was to hire a CTO
  and CMO. That capability already existed under different names. Check for duplication before
  headcount.
